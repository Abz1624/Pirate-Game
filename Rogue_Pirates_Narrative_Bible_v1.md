# Rogue Pirates — Narrative Bible

**Studio:** X2C  
**Version:** 1.1  
**Date:** 14 September 2026  
**Status:** Narrative and character baseline

---

# 1. Narrative Premise

**Rogue Pirates** is a world full of ambitious, greedy, stubborn, and slightly ridiculous pirates.

Everyone comes to these seas with roughly the same dream:

> **Get rich enough to retire.**

Nobody retires.

Captains sail out looking for Gold, treasure, territory, and bigger victories. During a voyage, their ship grows from a basic setup into an increasingly ridiculous combat machine through Weapons, Items, Logbooks, and upgrades.

Eventually, the ship is overwhelmed and sinks.

The Captain survives and returns home with the permanent rewards and Gold secured during the voyage.

But the temporary combat build is gone.

The next voyage starts again from Level 0.

> **You keep the fortune. You lose the build.**

The player's Territory, collection, wealth, Crew, cosmetics, achievements, and reputation keep growing even though every individual voyage begins fresh.

---

# 2. Tone

Rogue Pirates should feel:

- adventurous
- funny
- dangerous
- charming
- chaotic
- character-driven

The world can take danger seriously.

The characters don't always take themselves seriously.

Most comedy should come from personality rather than random jokes.

The Captains are not incompetent. They are capable people with very specific flaws that repeatedly get them into trouble.

The central recurring situation is simple:

**The sensible thing would be to stop.**

They usually don't.

---

# 3. The World

The seas are filled with rival pirates, ruined harbors, strange islands, treasure, old naval forces, dangerous creatures, abandoned wrecks, and Territories worth stealing from.

Players gradually establish and expand their own permanent Territory through:

1. **Saltwind**
2. **Gravewater**
3. **Devil's Maw**

The player's home remains recognizable as it grows.

Raiding another player's Territory creates an unpredictable route using recognizable parts of that player's Territory and defenses.

The intended fantasy is:

> **Every Territory can become a roguelike level created from another player's choices.**

The defender chooses the ingredients.

The raid creates uncertainty from them.

---

# 4. The Sea Keeps Receipts

**The Sea Keeps Receipts** is a saying and background theme rather than a complicated supernatural rule.

Its meaning is simply:

> Greed eventually catches up with you.

It can appear in old pirate sayings, environmental details, loading screens, Penny's warnings, boss dialogue, or marketing.

We do not need the sea itself to literally explain respawning, map generation, or every roguelike mechanic.

Ships sink.

Captains survive.

They rebuild.

Then they go looking for more.

---

# 5. Player Identity and Captain Identity

The six playable Captains are named characters with their own personalities and gameplay styles.

The player is not literally Rook, Hank, Richie, Sparky, Ember, or Roxie.

The player's long-term identity comes from their:

- Territory
- Crew
- collection
- Treasury
- ship cosmetics
- flags
- decorations
- titles
- achievements
- reputation
- leaderboard history
- player-created pirate persona

This separation lets the Captains become recognizable entertainment characters while still giving the player room to create an identity of their own.

---

# 6. The Voyage Loop in Fiction

## Start

The selected Captain begins at Level 0 with their signature starting Weapon and basic ship stats.

They retain access to permanent account progression, but the temporary build from the previous voyage is gone.

## During the Voyage

The Captain gains XP and builds the ship through randomized:

- Weapons
- Weapon upgrades
- Logbooks
- Items
- synergies
- temporary bonuses

Some builds fail.

Some become absurdly powerful.

## Boss Victory

Defeating the required boss secures the objective.

The run does not automatically end.

The player can continue fighting, collecting Gold, opening remaining chests, and pushing the build further.

This is where the pirate personality becomes especially important.

They already won.

But there is always something else worth risking the ship for.

## Sinking

When Hull reaches zero, the ship is wrecked.

The Captain survives.

The run ends.

## Returning Home

The player keeps permanent rewards and progression.

**All remaining run Gold is banked into the player's permanent wealth.**

Gold already spent during the voyage remains spent.

The temporary combat build disappears.

A new voyage begins from Level 0.

# 7. Main Cast

## Rook

**Role:** Generalist  
**Starting Weapon:** Deck Cannon  
**Hull:** 100  
**Speed:** 6.0 m/s

### Gameplay

Rook becomes gradually stronger as the run progresses.

His passive increases damage as he levels, up to the current prototype cap of +25%.

He is the easiest Captain to understand and the baseline for learning the game.

### Character

Rook wants to become a legendary pirate.

More importantly, he wants stories people will tell about him.

He is adventurous, charismatic, optimistic, impulsive, and ridiculously confident.

He is not incompetent. His dangerous plans work just often enough to convince him that all his plans are brilliant.

### Flaw

Rook values a great story more than a sensible decision.

### Comedy

His recurring attitude is:

> "Exactly as planned."

Especially when almost nothing happened according to plan.

### Why he keeps sailing

There is always one more story worth having.

---

## Hank

**Role:** Tank  
**Starting Weapon:** Deck Cannon  
**Hull:** 110  
**Speed:** 5.7 m/s

### Gameplay

Hank trades mobility for durability.

He gains Armor as he levels, making him the most forgiving Captain for players who prefer surviving damage rather than constantly avoiding it.

### Character

Hank is stubborn, dependable, practical, old-fashioned, and quietly protective.

He thinks dodging looks suspiciously similar to running away.

He understands that moving would often be strategically better.

He simply refuses on principle.

### Flaw

Once Hank decides to stand his ground, convincing him otherwise becomes almost impossible.

### Comedy

Roxie:

> "Dodge!"

Hank:

> "No."

### Human side

Hank is usually the person everyone wants nearby when something genuinely goes wrong.

He repairs things, protects people, and complains the entire time.

### Why he keeps sailing

Retiring sounds too much like quitting.

---

## Richie

**Role:** Smuggler  
**Starting Weapon:** Broadside  
**Hull:** 90  
**Speed:** 6.0 m/s

### Gameplay

Richie earns increased Gold pickup value as he levels.

His unspent run Gold also increases his damage.

The richer he becomes during a voyage, the stronger he becomes.

His Broadside fires from both sides of the ship, making positioning especially important.

### Character

Richie is charming, clever, stylish, persuasive, and pathologically reluctant to spend money.

He can sell someone their own stolen property and somehow make them feel like they got a deal.

### Flaw

Richie struggles to spend Gold even when spending it is obviously the smart thing to do.

### Human side

Richie grew up with very little.

Gold represents security as much as wealth.

He never wants to feel powerless again.

### Comedy

Penny:

> "The ship needs repairs."

Richie:

> "Agreed."

Penny:

> "They cost 300 Gold."

Richie:

> "The ship looks fine."

### Why he keeps sailing

There is always more Gold.

---

## Sparky

**Role:** Powderrunner  
**Starting Weapon:** Powder Kegs  
**Hull:** 90  
**Speed:** 6.3 m/s

### Gameplay

Sparky is built around continuous movement and explosives.

Kegs are deployed behind the ship, encouraging the player to kite enemies through dangerous routes.

Maintaining speed increases Keg damage.

### Character

Sparky is enthusiastic, restless, inventive, technically brilliant, and dangerously curious.

He understands explosives extremely well.

That is exactly why everyone else is worried.

### Flaw

His definition of an acceptable blast radius is very different from everyone else's.

### Comedy

Sparky:

> "I have an idea."

Everyone leaves.

### Example requisition

- 400 barrels of gunpowder
- 1 rope
- 0 lifeboats

Penny:

> "Why zero lifeboats?"

Sparky:

> "Budget cuts."

### Why he keeps sailing

There is always one more invention to test.

---

## Ember

**Role:** Firekeeper  
**Starting Weapon:** Flamethrower  
**Hull:** 90  
**Speed:** 6.0 m/s

### Gameplay

Ember improves burn damage as she levels.

Repeated burning kills also provide small amounts of recovery.

Her Flamethrower rewards careful facing and sustained positioning.

### Character

Ember is patient, caring, observant, soft-spoken, and disturbingly comfortable around fire.

She is not another chaos character.

Sparky likes explosions.

Ember likes control.

### Flaw

She has spent so much time around controlled burning that she no longer understands why other people find it alarming.

### Comedy

Ember talks about warfare like gardening or cooking.

> "Let it spread."

> "Needs another minute."

> "There. Much better."

### Human side

She genuinely looks after the crew and may be the warmest person in the group.

### Why she keeps sailing

Something always needs tending.

---

## Roxie

**Role:** Stormchaser  
**Starting Weapon:** Lightning Mast  
**Hull:** 85  
**Speed:** 6.3 m/s

### Gameplay

Roxie is fast and fragile.

Lightning damage grows with level, while narrowly avoiding enemy attacks temporarily increases her damage further.

Her gameplay rewards confident movement close to danger.

### Character

Roxie is competitive, energetic, impatient, thrill-seeking, and extremely confident in her own reflexes.

She isn't trying to die.

She wants to know exactly how close she can get without losing control.

### Flaw

A situation doesn't become interesting to Roxie until something is genuinely at risk.

### Comedy

A cannonball misses her by centimeters.

Everyone panics.

Roxie smiles.

> "Again."

### Why she keeps sailing

There is always a harder challenge.

---

# 8. Penny

**Role:** Quartermaster

Penny manages the parts of piracy the Captains would otherwise ignore:

- Treasury
- Captain's Ledger
- contracts
- upgrades
- logistics
- budgets
- Territory operations
- repair costs

She is practical, intelligent, organized, patient, dry, and increasingly tired.

Penny's comedy comes from being the one person reacting appropriately to completely unreasonable situations.

She should not become another chaos character.

She exists to make the chaos around her funnier.

### Example

Penny:

> "Good news. We made 2,400 Gold."

Everyone cheers.

Penny turns the page.

> "Repairs cost 2,650."

Silence.

Penny genuinely cares about the Captains, although most of that affection appears as frustration and damage control.

---

# 9. The Mascot

**Species:** Chipmunk  
**Name:** TBD

The mascot needs only one defining instinct:

> **If it's shiny, it goes in the cheeks.**

The chipmunk is tiny, fast, mostly silent, shameless, curious, and almost impossible to kill.

It does not deliberately cause disasters.

It simply sees something shiny and forgets everything else.

### Common targets

- Gold
- rings
- keys
- gems
- medals
- buttons
- compass parts
- Penny's pen
- Richie's coins
- boss artifacts
- Sparky's unfortunately shiny equipment

### Visual comedy

Its cheeks become absurdly large as it steals things.

Often the audience should know the chipmunk is guilty before the characters do.

### Richie relationship

Richie carefully tracks every coin.

The chipmunk steals coins.

This should become one of the strongest recurring comedy pairings in the franchise.

### Survivability

Ships sink.

Explosions happen.

Bosses destroy everything.

Afterward, the chipmunk crawls out carrying one shiny object.

Never explain how.

# 10. Core Character Relationships

The cast should generate stories naturally by pairing conflicting personalities.

| Characters | Dynamic |
|---|---|
| Rook × Penny | Confidence vs reality |
| Hank × Roxie | Refuses to dodge vs refuses to stop dodging |
| Richie × Chipmunk | Wealth preservation vs compulsive theft |
| Sparky × Ember | Chaotic destruction vs controlled destruction |
| Rook × Richie | Glory vs profit |
| Hank × Sparky | Repairs the ship vs destroys the ship |
| Penny × Richie | Necessary spending vs never spending |
| Penny × Sparky | Responsible procurement vs explosives wishlist |
| Ember × Roxie | Patience vs impatience |

Penny connects the whole group because she is responsible for keeping their operation functional.

# 11. Boss Characters

Bosses need simple, recognizable personalities rather than complicated backstories.

## Barnacle King

**Zone:** Saltwind

A petty territorial ruler who believes everything in Saltwind legally belongs to him.

The player's Territory is his property.

Their ship is improperly docked.

Their treasure is unpaid tax.

The chipmunk is unlicensed wildlife.

He treats piracy like a property dispute.

---

## Drowned Admiral

**Zone:** Gravewater

An undead naval officer still enforcing procedures from a government that no longer exists.

He is obsessed with:

- regulations
- naval etiquette
- permits
- orders
- forms
- procedure

Penny is one of the only people willing to argue regulations with him.

She hates every second of it.

---

## Infernal Kraken

**Zone:** Devil's Maw

An ancient monster that sees pirate fleets as minor annoyances.

The Captains believe fighting the Kraken is a legendary rivalry.

The Kraken barely knows who they are.

Rook:

> "We meet again."

The Kraken stares.

Rook:

> "...You remember me."

The humor should never make the Kraken feel harmless.

# 12. Narrative Delivery In-Game

Rogue Pirates should remain gameplay-first.

Narrative appears around existing milestones rather than constantly interrupting runs.

## Opening

A short, skippable introduction establishes:

- pirates come here to get rich
- they don't know when to stop
- ships sink
- they rebuild and go again

Keep it around 20 seconds.

## First Territory Claim

After Saltwind is secured and the run eventually ends, Penny introduces the Territory and Ledger.

Keep the exchange short.

## Zones

Each Zone needs:

- one short introduction
- one first-conquest aftermath

No large campaign chapter is required.

## During Runs

Use occasional character barks, reactions, Item flavor, boss lines, and environmental details.

Never interrupt combat for unnecessary dialogue.

## Results

The results screen is one of the best places for personality.

It can highlight:

- objective secured
- Gold earned
- build
- time survived
- boss kills
- cause of sinking
- how long the Captain stayed after already winning

These statistics can later feed directly into social content generation.

---

# 13. Dialogue Rules

Dialogue should be:

- short
- conversational
- character-specific
- easy to localize
- readable without voice acting

Avoid heavy pirate dialect.

Avoid constant "arr" jokes.

Avoid long lore explanations.

Avoid characters describing game systems unnaturally.

The player should be able to recognize who said a line even without seeing the character name.

---

# 14. Content and Marketing Strategy

The characters are not only there to support the game.

They should become an entertainment layer that helps players discover and share Rogue Pirates.

There are three connected content systems.

---

## 14.1 Official Character Content

X2C creates recurring short-form content using the canonical cast.

Examples:

### Rook's Great Plans

Rook explains why an obviously dangerous idea will definitely work.

### Penny's Expense Reports

An epic adventure ends with Penny calculating whether anyone actually made money.

### Richie vs the Chipmunk

Richie creates increasingly ridiculous ways to protect his Gold.

None work.

### Sparky Explains

Sparky calmly teaches something that becomes increasingly dangerous.

### Ember's Tips

Looks like gardening, cooking, or household advice.

Actually describes setting enemy fleets on fire.

### Hank vs Roxie

One refuses to move.

The other refuses to stop moving.

These formats should work as:

- 5–15 second jokes
- short comics
- reels
- animated shorts
- stickers
- memes

---

# 15. Player AI Pirate Persona

A major growth loop for Rogue Pirates should let players create a recognizable **AI pirate version of themselves**.

This character represents the player inside Rogue Pirates' social and marketing world.

It is separate from the six playable Captains.

The player could create it using:

- a selfie or avatar
- chosen name
- visual preferences
- Crew
- flag
- cosmetics
- Territory identity

The system transforms them into a character that visually belongs in the Rogue Pirates universe.

This persona does **not** gain exclusive gameplay abilities.

Its purpose is identity, entertainment, and sharing.

---

# 16. AI-Generated Gameplay Stories

The player's actual runs can become personalized stories starring their AI pirate persona.

The game already generates useful narrative information:

- selected Captain
- Weapons
- Items
- unusual build combinations
- Gold collected
- boss defeated
- damage taken
- near misses
- biggest kill streak
- time survived
- time spent after boss victory
- final cause of sinking
- Territory or Crew identity

Instead of presenting this only as statistics, AI can turn selected runs into entertainment.

## Output formats

### Short Comic

3–6 panels telling the story of the run.

### Short Video

Roughly 10–30 seconds.

Suitable for:

- TikTok
- X
- Instagram Reels
- Discord
- YouTube Shorts

### Illustrated Run Recap

A single shareable image summarizing the most interesting event.

### Character Reaction

One of the canonical characters reacts to what the player did.

Examples:

Penny reviews the player's spending.

Richie reacts to how much Gold they lost or saved.

Sparky reacts to an explosive build.

Roxie reacts to a ridiculous near miss.

---

# 17. Example AI Story

A player's run contains:

- Rook
- boss defeated at 10:22
- 2,400 Gold
- stayed another 3 minutes
- chased one remaining chest
- ship sank before reaching it

Instead of a normal results card, the player could generate:

### Panel 1

Their AI pirate persona and Rook celebrate defeating the boss.

Penny:

> "We're done. Come home."

### Panel 2

The player notices a chest in the distance.

Rook looks at them.

They both smile.

### Panel 3

Penny:

> "Don't."

### Panel 4

Wreckage floating in the ocean.

### Panel 5

Results:

**Extra Gold recovered:** 0  
**Ship recovered:** 0  
**Chest recovered:** 0

This is generated from something the player actually did.

That makes it personal and naturally shareable.

# 18. The AI Viral Loop

The intended loop is:

**Play the game**

↓

**Something funny or impressive happens**

↓

**Game records the important events**

↓

**AI turns the run into a comic, short video, or character moment starring the player's pirate persona**

↓

**Player shares it**

↓

**Friends discover Rogue Pirates through entertainment rather than an advertisement**

↓

**They create their own pirate persona and play**

↓

**Their runs create new stories**

This turns gameplay itself into a content generator.

The more unusual the run, the better the story.

# 19. Which Rogue Are You?

Before someone even plays the game, we can offer a lightweight character experience.

## Which Rogue Captain Are You?

Users answer a few funny situational questions.

Example:

**Your ship is sinking. What do you save?**

- The crew → Hank
- The Gold → Richie
- The gunpowder → Sparky
- "Why are we leaving?" → Rook
- Finish burning the enemy ship → Ember
- Ask how close we are to sinking → Roxie

The result assigns them a Captain personality.

AI then creates their own Rogue Pirates-styled version inspired by that archetype.

Example:

> **You're 82% Richie.**

Then:

**Create your pirate.**

The generated pirate becomes the user's social persona and gives them a direct reason to enter the game.

This product should work as entertainment even before the user becomes a player.

# 20. AI Content Guardrails

Personalized AI content must strengthen the main IP rather than replace it.

The six Captains retain fixed personalities.

AI should not rewrite Rook, Hank, Richie, Sparky, Ember, Roxie, or Penny differently for every user.

The player's AI pirate is the customizable layer.

Generated content should use real run information where presented as a gameplay recap.

It should not invent:

- Weapons the player never used
- bosses they never defeated
- rewards they never earned
- nonexistent mechanics
- false leaderboard results

Creative exaggeration is fine.

Misrepresenting gameplay is not.

Sharing should always be optional.

---

# 21. Content Flywheel

Rogue Pirates should gradually create three complementary kinds of entertainment:

### Canon Content

Stories about:

Rook, Hank, Richie, Sparky, Ember, Roxie, Penny, and the chipmunk.

Builds familiarity with the IP.

### Player Stories

AI-generated comics and shorts based on real gameplay.

Creates personalization and sharing.

### Community Stories

The funniest, strongest, strangest, or most disastrous player runs can become official community content.

Examples:

- most ridiculous build of the week
- greediest post-boss death
- closest Roxie-style near miss
- biggest Richie haul
- most chaotic Sparky run
- funniest AI comic

The boundary between playing the game and making content about the game should become very small.

# 22. Narrative Guardrails

1. **Captains survive sinking.** Mechanical death still ends the run.
2. **Boss defeat counts as success.** Dying later does not erase the objective.
3. **There is no voluntary extraction unless the game later adds one.**
4. **Intermediate portals continue the Voyage; they do not return the player home.**
5. **The player's home Territory remains recognizable.**
6. **Raid routes may rearrange recognizable Territory sections.**
7. **The six Captains are canonical characters, not blank avatars.**
8. **The player's customizable identity is separate from Captain identity.**
9. **Ship cosmetics remain cosmetic.**
10. **Narrative marketing should not imply mechanics that do not exist.**
11. **100% of remaining run Gold is banked when the run ends.**
12. **Temporary builds reset; permanent account progression does not.**

# 24. Narrative Goal

Rogue Pirates does not need enormous amounts of lore.

It needs characters players remember.

The long-term test is simple:

Players should know what is about to happen when:

- Rook sees another adventure
- Hank is told to dodge
- Richie leaves Gold unattended
- Sparky finds gunpowder
- Ember says "give it a minute"
- Roxie sees danger
- Penny receives the bill
- the chipmunk notices something shiny

Once those reactions become familiar, the cast can carry the game, comics, short videos, memes, social content, and player-generated AI stories without needing constant exposition.
