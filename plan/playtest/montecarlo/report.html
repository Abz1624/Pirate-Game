Saltwind Balance Simulator

A numbers-only Monte Carlo of a 15-minute Saltwind voyage, written from scratch and calibrated on your playtest. It plays a captain who collects 80% of the loot and fights 90% of the enemies, then measures damage, kills, XP, levels and hull minute by minute. The settings it recommended are now in the game; the live simulator below lets you try others.

Verdict

What the simulation found

  • Updated for round 7 (3 Oct, afternoon). The data is the game as it is now:
    • Flamethrower switched off (Ember starts with Swivel Guns).
    • Enemy base damage +25% for every enemy, variant and elite.
    • Miniboss and elite fight-length floors halved: arena fights 6 s, scheduled elites 3 s and kill-band elites 2.5 s of the build's theoretical damage per second (elite hull ×6). The boss is unchanged at 19 s.
    • Levels 1 to 2 now cost 5 XP, the same as 2 to 3.
  • Fights now: the crab and the scheduled elites take a median of 9 to 11 s, kill-band elites about 8 s and the Barnacle King about 37 s (see the Fights tab below).
  • The model still finds the voyage easy. On your profile (80% of the loot, 90% of the enemies engaged), 97% of voyages beat the boss, under 1% sink before 10:00 and the hull rarely drops below half. Treat this as a floor on difficulty: in round 6 the model also could not reproduce sinkings at 3 to 4 minutes, so real play is harder than it shows.
  • Most damage to you comes from elite Cutthroat Skiffs, the Barnacle King, elite Manta Rays and Drowned Harriers (see the Enemies tab below).
  • New: the full stat sheet of every weapon and enemy is further down.

How the model works

  • One step per second. Enemies spawn from the real timeline (bands, pools, floors, hordes, swarm pulses, the kill band, minibosses, the boss at 14:00, Drowned Pursuit after 15:00) using the real HP, damage and XP.
  • The player's weapons, levels, upgrade cards, Logbooks, chest items and evolutions follow the game's tables. Each weapon's hits per cycle and crowd reach come from a review of its code.
  • Fitted to your playtest: about 30% of the build's theoretical damage lands, a new enemy needs about 4 s to come into range, and the floor tops up about 0.5 enemies a second.
  • Incoming damage: melee contacts grow with the crowd, and shooters fire every 2.5 s with a hit chance. Island bumps are not modelled.
  • Limits: it is a guide, not the game. It sinks early voyages more often than you did, so the real game should land a little easier than these numbers. Play is the final check.
